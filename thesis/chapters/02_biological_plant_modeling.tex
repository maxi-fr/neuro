\chapter{Biological Plant Modeling}
\label{ch:biological_plant_modeling}

This chapter defines the biophysical plant used to test closed-loop seizure control.
The whole brain is represented as a network of $N=76$ regions, each modeled by one Jansen-Rit neural mass node.
The nodes interact through a structural connectome with conduction delays, while regional excitability determines where seizures begin and spread.
The chapter also specifies how scalp electrodes measure and stimulate the network, how the equations are integrated, and how the model parameters are chosen.

\section{The Jansen-Rit Neural Mass Model}

A neural mass model abandons the individual neuron and describes a cortical macro-column by the mean membrane potential and mean firing rate of whole populations.
This approximation is justified when the extracellular field is a spatial average over millions of cells~\cite{LopesDaSilva1974,Buzsaki2012}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Originally introduced to explain thalamic alpha rhythms~\cite{LopesDaSilva1974}, the formalism was given its three-population form by Jansen and Rit. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
They demonstrated that a single parameter set reproduces both resting alpha activity and visual evoked potentials~\cite{Jansen1995}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
The Jansen-Rit model combines physiological interpretability with the capacity to produce the spike-wave waveforms characteristic of epileptic seizures~\cite{Wendling2002,Yu2024}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
This distinguishes it from simpler two-state models that collapse synaptic filtering and fail to reproduce realistic seizure morphologies~\cite{David2003}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED

Each node $i$ represents one brain region by three interacting populations: \acp{py}, \acp{ein}, and \acp{iin}, wired as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:microcircuit}.
The excitatory branch forms a positive feedback loop and the inhibitory branch a negative one.
The competition between the two produces the oscillations the model is built to explain.
The strengths of the four intra-columnar projections are the average synaptic contact numbers $c_1$ to $c_4$, held at standard proportions $c_2 = 0.8\,c_1$ and $c_3 = c_4 = 0.25\,c_1$.
Thus, a single parameter $c_1$ scales the entire microcircuit~\cite{Jansen1995}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED

The \ac{py} population excites the \acp{ein} through $c_1$ and the \acp{iin} through $c_3$ and receives excitatory feedback through $c_2$ and inhibitory feedback through $c_4$. Every population converts its input in two stages.
The first is a linear pulse-to-wave operator turning an incoming mean firing rate into an average \ac{psp} by convolution with the impulse response
%
\begin{equation}
    h_\mathrm{e}(t) = A\,a\,t\,\mathrm{e}^{-a t}, \qquad
    h_\mathrm{i}(t) = B\,b\,t\,\mathrm{e}^{-b t}, \qquad t \geq 0 \,,
    \label{eq:psp_kernel}
\end{equation}
%
in which the synaptic gains $A$ and $B$ set the amplitude of the excitatory and inhibitory \ac{psp} in \si{\milli\volt} and the rate constants $a$ and $b$ set the reciprocal lumped time constants of dendritic delay and membrane decay.
The second stage is a static wave-to-pulse operator mapping the mean membrane potential $v$ of a population onto its mean firing rate through the sigmoid
%
\begin{equation}
    S(v) = \frac{2 e_0}{1 + \exp\!\big(r (v_0 - v)\big)} \,,
    \label{eq:sigmoid}
\end{equation}
%
whose ceiling $2 e_0$ in~\eqref{eq:sigmoid} is the maximum population firing rate, whose midpoint $v_0$ is the potential at which half the population fires and whose steepness $r$ reflects the spread of firing thresholds across the cells of the population.

The impulse responses in~\eqref{eq:psp_kernel} are the Green's functions of second-order linear operators, so each convolution can be written as a pair of first-order differential equations.
Together the three populations give the six states $x_{i,1}$ to $x_{i,6}$ per node.
The potentials $x_{i,1}$, $x_{i,2}$ and $x_{i,3}$ are the \ac{psp} of the pyramidal, excitatory and inhibitory branches and $x_{i,4}$, $x_{i,5}$ and $x_{i,6}$ are their respective time derivatives.
The dynamics of node $i$ have the state-space form
%
\begin{align}
    \dot{x}_{i,1} &= x_{i,4}, \nonumber \\
    \dot{x}_{i,4} &= A_i a\, S\!\left(x_{i,2} - x_{i,3} + s_i\right) - 2 a\, x_{i,4} - a^2 x_{i,1}, \nonumber \\
    \dot{x}_{i,2} &= x_{i,5}, \nonumber \\
    \dot{x}_{i,5} &= A_i a \left[ I + c_2 S\!\left(c_1 x_{i,1}\right) + I_{\mathrm{coup},i} + \zeta_i(t) \right] - 2 a\, x_{i,5} - a^2 x_{i,2} \,, \label{eq:jr_node} \\
    \dot{x}_{i,3} &= x_{i,6}, \nonumber \\
    \dot{x}_{i,6} &= B b\, c_4 S\!\left(c_3 x_{i,1}\right) - 2 b\, x_{i,6} - b^2 x_{i,3} \,, \nonumber
\end{align}
%
where $I$ is the constant excitatory background input, $I_{\mathrm{coup},i}$ is the net drive from other nodes, and $s_i$ is the externally applied electrical stimulation drive.
The term $\zeta_i(t)$ is zero-mean Gaussian process representing stochastic fluctuations in afferent firing rate, parameterized by diffusion intensity $\sigma$ and independent across nodes.
For nodes near a bifurcation, this noise can trigger transitions from a resting state to an oscillating state.
It therefore ensures that simulated seizures start at irregular times and that consecutive runs differ naturally~\cite{Yu2024}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED

The quantity read out of a node is the net depolarisation of the pyramidal population,
%
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{LFP},i}(t) = x_{i,2}(t) - x_{i,3}(t) \,,
    \label{eq:lfp}
\end{equation}
%
which represents the difference between the excitatory and inhibitory \ac{psp} arriving at the pyramidal soma.
The regional \ac{lfp} proxy in~\eqref{eq:lfp} forms the basis for the simulated extracellular field.
In the absence of stimulation, this same potential drives the pyramidal sigmoid in~\eqref{eq:jr_node}; stimulation shifts its argument by $s_i$.

\section{Connectome Coupling and Conduction Delays}
\label{sec:connectome}

Scaling from an isolated column to a whole-brain simulation involves specifying the topology and timing of inter-regional communication.
The structural connectivity matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ dictates this architecture over the $N=76$ regions provided by the default cortical parcellation of \ac{tvb}~\cite{SanzLeon2013}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
The matrix entry $w_{ij}$ quantifies the density of connecting fibres between regions $i$ and $j$.
Because diffusion-weighted tractography recovers fibre bundles without distinguishing their direction, the connectivity matrix is symmetric.
A single global coupling factor $K$ scales the entire network to balance local and global dynamics.

Because long-range cortico-cortical projections are carried by axons, the transmitted signal is a firing rate rather than a potential and its arrival is delayed by finite conduction velocities.
The physical conduction delay is $d_{ij}^{\ast}=\ell_{ij}/c$, where $\ell_{ij}$ is the tract length and $c$ is an assumed uniform conduction speed.
The simulation stores an integer number of plant steps, $n_{ij}=\operatorname{round}(d_{ij}^{\ast}/\Delta t_\mathrm{p})$, and uses $d_{ij}=n_{ij}\Delta t_\mathrm{p}$ in the delay matrix $\mathbf{D}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$.
Here $\Delta t_\mathrm{p}=\SI{0.1}{\milli\second}$, so rounding changes a physical delay by at most half a plant step.
The delayed presynaptic firing from connected regions enters the excitatory \ac{psp} channel of the receiving node.
Thus, the total incoming network coupling term in~\eqref{eq:jr_node} takes the form
%
\begin{equation}
    I_{\mathrm{coup},i}(t) = K \sum_{j=1}^{N} w_{ij}\, S\!\big(y_{\mathrm{LFP},j}(t - d_{ij})\big) \,.
    \label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
%
Equations~\eqref{eq:jr_node} and~\eqref{eq:coupling} specify a stochastic delay system with the full state $\mathbf{x}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{6N}$.
With tract lengths on the order of \SI{100}{\milli\metre} and conduction speeds near \SI{50}{\milli\metre\per\milli\second}, inter-regional delays span several milliseconds.
These delays are comparable to synaptic time constants.
Consequently, they shape the phase relationships governing whether an actively seizing region recruits its neighbors~\cite{SanzLeon2015}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED

The plant advances with a stochastic Heun step of $\Delta t_\mathrm{p}=\SI{0.1}{\milli\second}$, giving 100 integration steps per \SI{10}{\milli\second} excitatory synaptic time constant.
Let $\mathbf{x}_{i,k}=(x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,6})^\mathsf{T}$ be the state of node $i$ at step $k$, and let $\mathbf{f}_{i,k}(\mathbf{x})$ denote the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:jr_node} without the stochastic term $A_i a\,\zeta_i(t)$, with coupling and stimulation evaluated at that step.  % this is wrong. Because of the delays we need to also keep in mind the past states
Integration of the afferent noise term over one step gives the discrete increment entering the excitatory velocity state $x_{i,5}$:
%
\begin{equation}
    \xi_{i,k} = \int_{t_k}^{t_k+\Delta t_\mathrm{p}}\!A_i a\,\zeta_i(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
    = A_i a\,\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t_\mathrm{p}}\,Z_{i,k},  % maybe theres a nicer more standard variable than Z that we could use here?
    \qquad Z_{i,k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0, 1) \,.
    \label{eq:plant_noise_increment}
\end{equation}
%
The draws $Z_{i,k}$ are independent across nodes and steps; hence $\xi_{i,k}$ in~\eqref{eq:plant_noise_increment} has variance $(A_i a\,\sigma)^2\Delta t_\mathrm{p}$.
Writing $\mathbf{e}_5$ for the fifth unit vector, the predictor and corrector are
%
\begin{align}
    \widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i,k+1}
        &= \mathbf{x}_{i,k} + \Delta t_\mathrm{p}\,\mathbf{f}_{i,k}(\mathbf{x}_{i,k})
           + \xi_{i,k}\mathbf{e}_5, \label{eq:plant_heun_predictor} \\
    \mathbf{x}_{i,k+1}
        &= \mathbf{x}_{i,k} + \frac{\Delta t_\mathrm{p}}{2}
           \left[\mathbf{f}_{i,k}(\mathbf{x}_{i,k})
           + \mathbf{f}_{i,k}(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i,k+1})\right]
           + \xi_{i,k}\mathbf{e}_5 \,.
           \label{eq:plant_heun_corrector}
\end{align}
%
In~\eqref{eq:plant_heun_predictor} and~\eqref{eq:plant_heun_corrector}, coupling and stimulation are held fixed across both evaluations of $\mathbf{f}_{i,k}$, and the same noise sample appears in both stages.

\section{Parameter selection}
\label{sec:parameter_selection}

To place seizure onset in selected regions, the excitatory synaptic gain varies between nodes and is written $A_i$.
The other local parameters in~\eqref{eq:jr_node} are shared across the network.
Increasing $A_i$ strengthens the excitatory loop relative to the inhibitory one.
As $A_i$ rises, the node undergoes a Hopf bifurcation from a stable fixed point to a large-amplitude limit cycle characterized by spike-wave morphology.

Following the excitability partition for whole-brain epilepsy models in~\cite{Yu2024}, three distinct levels are configured. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Healthy background tissue takes $A_i = 3.25\,\si{\milli\volt}$ and rests at the stable fixed point.
The \ac{ez} takes $A_i = 3.6\,\si{\milli\volt}$ and oscillates autonomously, needing no external network input to spontaneously seize.
Finally, the \ac{pz} takes an intermediate value of $A_i = 3.4\,\si{\milli\volt}$, leaving it just below its own bifurcation threshold but sufficiently excitable that coupling from an active \ac{ez} can readily recruit it into the seizure.
Background nodes have a lower gain and are less susceptible to the same incoming drive, although sufficiently strong network activity can recruit them too.

Figure~\ref{fig:bifurcation} compares the isolated regimes and shows how network coupling recruits a \ac{pz} node.

The anatomical assignment follows the left mesial temporal configuration of the whole-brain reference model~\cite{Yu2024}.
The \ac{ez} is defined in the left hemisphere encompassing the hippocampus, parahippocampal cortex and amygdala.
These deeply situated limbic structures are highly interconnected and frequently serve as the initial generators of seizure activity.
The immediate \ac{pz} is configured in the adjacent left inferior temporal cortex and left ventral temporal cortex.
This partition reflects the typical clinical pattern where paroxysmal activity originates in mesial temporal structures before spreading outward to lateral temporal neocortex.
The difference between the isolated and coupled \ac{pz} traces identifies the propagation that the controller must prevent.

Table~\ref{tab:jr_params} collects the local Jansen-Rit parameters, regional gains and network parameters used throughout the simulations.
The shared synaptic gains, time constants, sigmoid parameters and background input follow the original Jansen-Rit fit~\cite{Jansen1995}.
The conduction speed $c$ fixes the tract-length delays described in Section~\ref{sec:connectome}.

The coupling factor $K$ and noise intensity $\sigma$ were then adjusted for the 76-region connectome and the selected left temporal \ac{ez} and \ac{pz}.
Increasing $K$ strengthens the drive into connected regions, whereas increasing $\sigma$ makes noise-triggered transitions more frequent.
The target behavior was seizure onset in the \ac{ez}, subsequent recruitment of the \ac{pz} and much of the left hemisphere, and limited early spread into the right hemisphere.
The roughly \SI{10}{\second} progression shown in the published uncontrolled traces informed this qualitative target~\cite{Yu2024}.
The values used here are $K=0.60$ and $\sigma=0.86\,\si{\second^{-1/2}}$, which corresponds to an effective noise increment of $A_i a\,\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t_\mathrm{p}} \approx 280\sqrt{\Delta t_\mathrm{p}}$ on baseline healthy tissue ($A_i = 3.25\,\si{\milli\volt}$).
Over the $\Delta t_\mathrm{p} = \SI{0.1}{\milli\second}$ integration step, this continuous diffusion intensity translates to an instantaneous discrete pulse-density standard deviation of $\sigma/\sqrt{\Delta t_\mathrm{p}} \approx 86\,\si{\per\second}$, on par with the mean background drive $I$.

\section{Electrical Interfaces}

While the delay differential equations describe the hidden biological states, a closed-loop controller can only interact with the brain through physical interfaces.
The control problem is defined by the limited spatial footprint of these sensory and actuation pathways.

\subsection{\acs{eeg} Measurement}

The regional variable $y_{\mathrm{LFP},i}$ represents the net depolarisation of the pyramidal population.
Biophysically, the synaptic currents entering the apical dendrites, along with the return currents at the soma, form a dipole oriented along the somato-dendritic axis of the cell.
Because pyramidal neurons are aligned perpendicularly to the cortical sheet, their dipoles sum coherently across the spatial extent of a region rather than cancelling out, dominating the extracellular field~\cite{Buzsaki2012}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
In the Jansen-Rit equations, $y_{\mathrm{LFP},i}$ is a difference of postsynaptic potentials and therefore has units of millivolts.
The model does not specify the geometry needed to convert this potential into an absolute dipole moment.
Here it is used as a regional source proxy, so the scalp amplitudes obtained from it have a model-dependent scale.

The signals measurable at the scalp are generated from these regional dipoles via volume conduction through the head tissues.
The quasi-static approximation of Maxwell's equations makes this relationship strictly linear~\cite{Buzsaki2012}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
%
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}_\mathrm{EEG}(t) = \mathbf{L}_\mathrm{EEG}\, \mathbf{y}_\mathrm{LFP}(t) \,,
    \label{eq:eeg}
\end{equation}
%
where the \ac{eeg} lead field $\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{EEG}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}$ in~\eqref{eq:eeg} is the linear map from regional source proxies to $M$ simulated scalp channels.
For this simulation, it is constructed from the surface projection used by \ac{tvb} onto 62 scalp positions with international 10-10-style electrode labels.
The original projection maps 16,384 cortical vertices to these 62 electrodes~\cite{SanzLeon2015}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Summing the columns belonging to each of the 76 anatomical regions collapses the operator to the required $62 \times 76$ dimensions.
Because the input to the projection is a model potential rather than a calibrated dipole moment, the amplitudes of $\mathbf{y}_\mathrm{EEG}$ cannot be interpreted as physical scalp voltages.
Signal thresholds and control costs in later chapters are therefore defined relative to the simulated healthy baseline.

\subsection{Stimulation Interface}

Therapeutic electrical actuation operates in the reverse direction.
Transcranial currents injected via scalp electrodes produce a widespread electric field inside the head.
The component of this field aligned with the somato-dendritic axis alters the transmembrane potential of the pyramidal cells.
This exogenous polarization shifts the operating point of the cellular population on its activation sigmoid without directly injecting a synaptic firing rate~\cite{Buzsaki2012}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Within the Jansen-Rit formulation, this macroscopic mechanism is modeled by introducing the additive stimulation drive $s_i$ inside the argument of the pyramidal sigmoid, as specified in~\eqref{eq:jr_node}~\cite{Kunze2016,Merlet2013,Yu2024}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED

We used ROAST with its standard MNI152 head model to compute the electric-field basis solutions~\cite{Huang2019}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Because its standardized cap has no TP9 position, we chose the nearby TPP9 position for that field calculation.
Collecting the electrode currents in $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{P}$, the regional membrane perturbations are
%
\begin{equation}
    s_i(t) = \sum_{p=1}^{P} [\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{stim}]_{ip}u_p(t),
    \qquad [\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{stim}]_{ip}
    = \lambda_\mathrm{p}\,\mathbf{E}_{p,i}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{n}_i \,,
    \label{eq:regional_stimulation_projection}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{stim}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times P}$ is the stimulation lead field, $\mathbf{E}_{p,i}$ is the field at the centroid of region $i$ for \SI{1}{\milli\ampere} at electrode $p$ against the Ex8 return, and $\mathbf{n}_i$ is the region's outward cortical normal vector.
The assumed somatic polarisation length $\lambda_\mathrm{p}=\SI{0.35}{\milli\metre}$ converts the normal electrical field component to a membrane-potential shift~\cite{Radman2009}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
The relation in~\eqref{eq:regional_stimulation_projection} also gives $\mathbf{s}(t)=\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{stim}\mathbf{u}(t)$ in millivolts.

A previous left temporal epilepsy model used a CP5 cathode with an Ex8 return~\cite{Yu2024}.
Our three-electrode search over 62 scalp positions and Ex8 selected TP9, CP5 and Ex8 as the best montage for targeted inhibitory drive in the \ac{pz}.
TP9 supplies a second independently adjustable scalp current near the left temporal cortex; the search procedure and results are reserved for the appendix.
The applied currents obey $|u_p|\leq\SI{2}{\milli\ampere}$ and Kirchhoff's current law, $\sum_{p=1}^{P}u_p=0$, so the electrodes inject no net current into the head at a given time.

Helmholtz reciprocity relates source measurements and fields from the same electrode pair when their source geometry and reference are matched~\cite[ch.~11]{Malmivuo1995}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
The two lead fields here do not meet those conditions: $\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{EEG}$ maps regional potential proxies through the surface projection used by \ac{tvb}, while $\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{stim}$ uses ROAST fields sampled at regional centroids and projected onto cortical normals.
Ex8 is a neck return and is absent from the 62-channel \ac{tvb} \ac{eeg} layout.
Consequently, $\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{stim}$ is not proportional to $\mathbf{L}_\mathrm{EEG}^{\mathsf{T}}$.

The resulting plant has $6N$ delayed stochastic states, $M$ simulated scalp measurements and $P$ stimulation currents.
The controller cannot observe the internal states directly.
Chapter~\ref{ch:data_driven_predictor_design} therefore deals with the design of data-driven prediction models from the scalp measurements which are then used in Chapter~\ref{ch:mpc_framework} to formulate the control policies that leverage them.
