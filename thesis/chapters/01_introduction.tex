\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}


Medication does not achieve seizure freedom for all patients with epilepsy, leaving a need for additional treatment options~\cite{Kwan2000,Chen2018}. % cite-checked: Kwan2000=SUPPORTED, Chen2018=SUPPORTED
Epileptic activity can appear in \ac{eeg} recordings as epileptiform spikes and high-frequency oscillations~\cite{Chang2020}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Electrical stimulation aims to suppress seizures by modulating neural activity, using implanted electrodes or non-invasive \ac{tes}~\cite{Morrell2011,Fregni2006,Auvichayapat2013}. % cite-checked: Morrell2011=SUPPORTED, Fregni2006=SUPPORTED, Auvichayapat2013=SUPPORTED
Clinical studies of cathodal transcranial direct current stimulation have used prescribed protocols with fixed electrode arrangements, current amplitudes, and durations~\cite{Fregni2006,Auvichayapat2013}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
These open-loop protocols do not adjust stimulation in response to ongoing neural activity during treatment~\cite{Fregni2006,Auvichayapat2013}. % cite-checked: Fregni2006=SUPPORTED, Auvichayapat2013=SUPPORTED
Closed-loop approaches instead use electrical recordings to detect seizure-related activity and modulate stimulation, as studied with implanted cortical electrodes in patients and with \ac{tes} in a rodent model~\cite{Morrell2011,Berenyi2012}. % cite-checked: Morrell2011=SUPPORTED, Berenyi2012=SUPPORTED
\Ac{mpc} uses a dynamical model to predict the effects of candidate stimulation sequences and optimizes future inputs according to a control objective and stimulation constraints~\cite{Rawlings2017,Chang2020}. % cite-checked: Rawlings2017=SUPPORTED, Chang2020=SUPPORTED

The prediction model must capture how the measured activity responds to stimulation~\cite{Chang2020}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Earlier applications include feedback control of single-neuron dynamics by Fr\"ohlich and Jezernik~\cite{Frohlich2005}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
For seizure suppression, Chang et al.~\cite{Chang2020} identify a second-order nonlinear autoregressive moving-average Volterra model from stimulation inputs and neural-mass outputs for use within \ac{mpc}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Liang et al.~\cite{Liang2024} combine dynamic mode decomposition with \ac{mpc} to investigate stimulation of selected nodes in an epileptic network. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
These studies demonstrate different routes to constructing prediction models for neural control.

Scalp stimulation produces spatially distributed electric fields, while scalp measurements reflect neural currents conducted through the head tissues~\cite{Huang2019,Buzsaki2012}. % cite-checked: Huang2019=SUPPORTED, Buzsaki2012=SUPPORTED
This thesis models how scalp-electrode currents produce regional stimulation and how regional activity appears in scalp measurements.
Several \ac{mpc} studies use direct inputs to neural populations or network nodes and regional activity as the output~\cite{Chang2020,Fang2023,Liang2024}. % cite-checked: Chang2020=SUPPORTED, Fang2023=SUPPORTED, Liang2024=SUPPORTED
The present work additionally models the mapping from scalp-electrode currents to regional stimulation and from regional activity to scalp measurements~\cite{Chang2020,Fang2023,Liang2024}. % cite-checked: Chang2020=SUPPORTED, Fang2023=SUPPORTED, Liang2024=SUPPORTED
Yu et al.~\cite{Yu2024} include electric-field modeling and \ac{eeg} feedback in a whole-brain study of closed-loop \ac{tes}, using a threshold-based feedback law. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Their stimulation model combines electric-field magnitude with a voltage-based polarity assignment.
The present work instead uses the field component along regional neural orientations and an explicit conversion from electrode currents to membrane-potential perturbations.
This mapping provides a more detailed stimulation interface for investigating \ac{mpc}.

Alongside these interfaces, the choice of prediction target determines the information available to the control objective.
Spectral representations have been used to distinguish preictal and interictal activity from \ac{eeg}, including the \ac{stft} inputs used by Truong et al.~\cite{Truong2018}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Fang and Yang~\cite{Fang2023} identify a dynamical model of regional spectral powers and use it within \ac{mpc} to regulate neural activity in a simulation study of major depressive disorder. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Steffen and Cannon~\cite{Steffen2025} predict the amplitude envelope of beta-band-filtered \acp{lfp} using a multi-step neural-network model within \ac{mpc} for deep brain stimulation in Parkinson's disease. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Each prediction step uses a difference of two input-convex neural networks, taking past measurements and stimulation inputs together with candidate future inputs~\cite{Steffen2025}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
Fang and Yang and Steffen and Cannon use derived measures of oscillatory activity as prediction targets within \ac{mpc}~\cite{Fang2023,Steffen2025}. % cite-checked: SUPPORTED
This thesis investigates direct prediction of \ac{stft}-derived \ac{eeg} spectra for seizure control and compares it with waveform-based prediction.
Predicting \ac{stft}-derived quantities can align the prediction target with a spectral control objective, rather than requiring the objective to be evaluated from predicted waveforms.

The control formulation must also determine when stimulation is applied.
Previous optimal-control studies have investigated energy and sparsity penalties for neural populations and whole-brain networks~\cite{Chouzouris2021,Salfenmoser2022,Salfenmoser2023}. % cite-checked: Chouzouris2021=SUPPORTED, Salfenmoser2022=SUPPORTED, Salfenmoser2023=SUPPORTED
This thesis compares continuous formulations that promote sparse currents with mixed-integer formulations that explicitly select stimulation intervals.
The resulting trade-off between control performance, stimulation expenditure, and computational cost is a further focus of this thesis.

This thesis brings together the stimulation and measurement interfaces, spectral prediction, and sparse stimulation within a simulation study of \ac{mpc} for epilepsy treatment.
A biophysical whole-brain model supplies the stimulation responses and scalp \ac{eeg} used to identify prediction models and evaluate feedback control.
The controller uses these identified models to optimize electrode currents, with waveform and spectral prediction providing alternative representations of future activity.
The three contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Application of \ac{mpc} with realistic stimulation and \ac{eeg} measurement models, including spatial electric-field distributions, orientation-dependent neural polarization, and constrained electrode currents.
    \item Direct prediction of \ac{stft}-derived spectral quantities for use within \ac{mpc}, and comparison with waveform-based prediction to investigate the effect of aligning the prediction target with the control objective.
    \item Formulation and comparison of sparsity-promoting \ac{mpc} approaches using mixed-integer stimulation scheduling and continuous relaxations.
\end{enumerate}

The following chapters develop and evaluate these contributions.
Chapter~\ref{ch:problem_formulation} establishes the biological and control setting, and Chapter~\ref{ch:biological_plant_modeling} specifies the biophysical plant.
Chapters~\ref{ch:data_driven_predictor_design} and~\ref{ch:mpc_framework} develop the prediction models and control formulations.
Chapter~\ref{ch:experiments_discussion} presents the experimental comparisons and discussion, followed by the conclusions in Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion_and_future_outlook}.
