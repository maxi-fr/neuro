# Experiment chapter checklist

Chapter 6 has three main questions to answer. **Can scalp-EEG MPC suppress seizures in the simulated plant? Does predicting log power directly improve control under a shared spectral objective? Does explicitly scheduling stimulation create more time off at comparable suppression and charge, and what does that cost to solve?** Chapters 4 and 5 also set up predictor-selection and solver comparisons that support those answers. This checklist draws on the other chapters and the abstract. Chapter 6 was excluded from the review. [Introduction, research questions and contributions](../thesis/chapters/01_introduction.tex#L46)

## 1. Does scalp-EEG MPC suppress seizures?

- **How do the two MPC variants compare with no stimulation and threshold-triggered stimulation?** The abstract commits to both waveform-based and direct spectral MPC reducing the average fraction of regions involved relative to **both** baselines, and delivering less charge than threshold-triggered stimulation. Report the paired differences across simulation seeds, their variability, and the threshold baseline’s trigger and current policy. [Abstract](../thesis/thesis.tex#L246)
- **Does MPC suppress activity in the epileptogenic zone (EZ), prevent recruitment outside it, or achieve partial suppression?** These are distinct outcomes in the problem formulation. Break out the EZ, propagation zone, and remaining regions. Distinguish regions active at a given time from regions ever recruited, so that delayed spread, reduced ongoing involvement, and prevention of spread can be interpreted separately. Use the stated regional criterion of peak-to-peak activity above 5 mV over the preceding second. [Problem formulation, control objective](../thesis/chapters/02_problem_formulation.tex#L45) · [Plant calibration and recruitment criterion](../thesis/chapters/03_biological_plant_modeling.tex#L294)
- **Does reducing the scalp-EEG objective correspond to reducing regional seizure activity?** Show how the measured spectral excess and regional activity evolve together, including any runs where they diverge. Regional outputs are available for evaluation, while feedback uses scalp EEG and applied currents. Verify the applied currents satisfy the ±2 mA electrode bounds and sum to zero at each interval. [Feedback setting](../thesis/chapters/02_problem_formulation.tex#L88) · [MPC costs and constraints](../thesis/chapters/05_mpc_framework.tex#L242)

## 3. What does explicit scheduling buy, and what does it cost?

- **What does the $L_1$ penalty contribute on its own?** Compare the continuous $L_2$ effort baseline with $L_2+L_1$ MPC. Measure how suppression, absolute delivered charge, and time with all electrodes off change. State the numerical tolerance used to classify an applied interval as off. [Current-effort and sparsity formulations](../thesis/chapters/05_mpc_framework.tex#L307)
- **What does interval activation add beyond $L_1$?** Compare $L_2+L_1$ with the same base objective plus the activation penalty. The abstract’s claim is specifically **more total time off at similar suppression and charge**. Quantify all three and show the associated solve-time cost. Report applied off-time separately from the enabled fraction in the predicted plan, since only the first current is applied before replanning. If the hard activation cap was evaluated, ask how its value changes the realized trade-off relative to the soft penalty. [Abstract](../thesis/thesis.tex#L251) · [Interval activation formulation](../thesis/chapters/05_mpc_framework.tex#L392)
- **How do the activation solvers compare?** Chapter 5 sets up Bonmin branch and bound, grouped candidate MPC, and continuous relaxation with homotopy. For methods tested on comparable instances, report solve-time distributions against the controller update interval, successful feasible solves, and achieved objective. Explain how block count affects candidate search and timing flexibility, and how often homotopy reaches binary tolerance. If a method times out or cannot support a closed-loop run, its observed solver behavior still answers part of the computational question. [Solution approaches](../thesis/chapters/05_mpc_framework.tex#L446)
- **Which numerical transcription is practical for these predictors?** Chapter 5 explicitly points forward to a simulation comparison of single shooting, full-state multiple shooting, and the reduced NARX transcription. Compare solve times, convergence, and achieved objective on the same predictor and control problem where those transcriptions were run. Chapter 5 already gives the variable and constraint counts. The remaining question is what those counts mean in practice. [Transcription counts](../thesis/chapters/05_mpc_framework.tex#L201) · [Chapter 5 summary](../thesis/chapters/05_mpc_framework.tex#L529)
